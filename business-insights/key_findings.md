# Key Findings

## 1. Strong overall profitability

The dataset generated ₹37.58 million in revenue and ₹10.07 million in profit, producing an overall observed profit margin of 26.78%.

## 2. Home & Kitchen is the largest revenue category

Home & Kitchen generated approximately ₹11.12 million in revenue and ₹2.80 million in profit. Electronics generated approximately ₹8.80 million in revenue and ₹2.53 million in profit.

Electronics had the highest observed category-level profit margin at about 28.71%, compared with 25.16% for Home & Kitchen.

## 3. Discounting is strongly associated with lower observed margins

Observed profit margin fell from 35.40% with no discount to 29.60% at 1–10%, 22.23% at 11–20%, and 11.28% at 21–30%.

This does not by itself establish causation, but it provides evidence that high discount bands deserve tighter margin controls.

## 4. Repeat purchasing is substantial

2,552 of the 3,921 purchasing customers were repeat customers, giving a repeat purchase rate of 65.09%.

This indicates that retention and reactivation analysis is an important part of the business problem.

## 5. RFM analysis identifies different customer priorities

Using the Power BI RFM rules, the customer base was segmented into Champions, Loyal Customers, Potential Loyalists, New Customers, At Risk and Lost Customers.

Champions contributed approximately ₹12.91 million in revenue and had the highest average customer value among the RFM segments.

The At Risk segment generated approximately ₹5.58 million, making reactivation a meaningful area for customer-management analysis.

## 6. Delivery reliability is a major operational issue

6,913 orders were On Time, 4,608 were Late and 479 were Not Delivered. Among delivered orders, the late-delivery rate was approximately 40.00%.

Shipping-mode analysis also showed different operational profiles: Standard averaged about 5.79 days with a 28.24% late rate, Express averaged about 3.74 days with a 56.80% late rate, and Same Day averaged about 2.01 days with a 68.50% late rate.

## 7. Returns are concentrated in identifiable reasons

There were 1,042 returned orders, representing an 8.68% return rate. Quality Issue was the largest return reason at 27.0% of returns, followed by Damaged at 22.0%, Changed Mind at 20.2%, Wrong Item at 15.6%, and Late Delivery at 15.3%.

## 8. Customer concentration is meaningful but not extreme

The top 20% of purchasing customers accounted for approximately 57.12% of total revenue. This supports targeted retention and high-value customer management, while also showing that revenue is not entirely dependent on a very small customer group.
